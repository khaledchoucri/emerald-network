Pokémon × Battle Network

Pokémon Emerald, with Mega Man Battle Network's real-time grid battles in place of Emerald's turn-based battles. Thanks for playtesting. This page has the controls, how battles work, and what to send back.

Getting started

  • In a browser (any computer, phone or tablet): open the link you were sent and click or tap to start. It was tested in Chrome; tell us how other browsers do.
  • Windows: unzip the folder somewhere you can write to (not Program Files), then run pokeemerald64.exe. Keep SDL2.dll next to it.
  • Mac: there's no Mac build yet. The Windows version runs through a Wine-based app such as CrossOver, Whisky or Porting Kit. Point it at pokeemerald64.exe.
  • Linux: build it with scripts/setup_host.sh from the project.

If your copy came with pkbn_b2_sprites.bin next to the game, the Pokémon in battles are animated.

Start a new game. Your first battle, saving Prof. Birch on Route 101, teaches the basics as you play.

Battles

Battles run in real time on a 3×6 grid. Your Pokémon stands on the red panels, the foe on the blue ones.

  1. Custom screen. Each battle opens with it. Your Pokémon's moves are chips, dealt from your folder.
    • Move the cursor with the +Control Pad and pick chips with A; B takes one back.
    • Chips you pick together must share a letter (their code) or be the same chip. A * goes with any letter, but only one * chip per turn.
    • A teammate's chip can't start a turn, but it can join a chain with the same letter: that teammate jumps in for the one move.
    • Once a battle, before you pick anything, R deals you a new hand.
    • Pick OK or press START to send them.
  2. Fight.
    • Move on your panels.
    • A uses the chip shown above your Pokémon. Each chip works once.
    • B fires your weak but quick Buster; hold it to keep firing.
    • Glowing panels warn where a foe's attack will land.
    • Wild Pokémon don't let you hide in your back row for long.
  3. Places. Where you fight changes the field. On beaches and the sea the tide washes over both back rows now and then. In caves rocks fall where a shadow grows, and stay in the way until you break them. Woods and long grass hide snares. In the rain, puddles slow you down and carry Electric attacks.
  4. Custom gauge. The bar at the top fills over about 8 seconds. When it's full, press L or R for a new hand.
  5. Speed matters. Faster Pokémon step quicker and recover from their moves sooner.
  6. Counter. Hit a foe while it's attacking to stun it. Your next chip then hits twice as hard, and your Custom gauge jumps a quarter.
  7. Program Advances. Some sets of three chips, picked one after another in the same Custom, combine into one big attack. Three different moves of one type, each at least as strong as the one before, always do (FIRE TRIO and so on). The rest are there to discover.
  8. More slots in Custom.
    • SW sends in another of your Pokémon.
    • The Poké Ball slot throws a ball in wild battles. L/R change the ball.
  9. Cross (after the third Gym badge). On the Custom screen, press Up from the top row and pick a Pokémon on your bench. For the rest of the battle your Pokémon takes its type and can use its chips. A hit that's super effective on that type breaks the Cross.
  10. Bonds and Unison. Pokémon that fight together grow closer: crossing with each other, switching in for each other, and winning together. The Cross window shows the pair's Bond. When it's full, their Cross becomes a Unison: your Pokémon takes on its partner's colors, and the pair's best two moves followed by any * chip make a Program Advance of their own.
  11. Dark Chips. Team Magma's and Team Aqua's admins and bosses offer one after you beat them. They are very strong, but the Pokémon that uses one loses friendship and Bond with the others, and Full Synchro, Cross and Unison are off for the rest of that battle.
  12. Beast Out (after you beat or catch a legendary). Press SELECT on the Custom screen. For 3 turns your chips hit harder, home in on the foe, and the Buster fires twice as fast. Doing it again in the same battle ends badly.
  13. Running. Hold SELECT for half a second during the fight, or press L on the Custom screen.

Some battles are still Emerald's own turn-based ones: Wally's catching demo, the Safari Zone, the Battle Frontier and similar facilities, and link battles.

Menus

  • FOLDER (START menu) is where you build your folder. L/R switch pages, and SELECT offers ready-made folders. REGULAR in that menu marks the highlighted chip to be in your first hand every battle.
  • NAVICUST (party menu) opens after your first Gym badge. Place programs on the board to power up a Pokémon:
    • L/R turn a program, and START runs the board.
    • To take a program off, press SELECT on it, or B while holding it.
  • PA DEX is the last page of FOLDER: every Program Advance you've found, and hints for the rest. Every fifth one you find brings a reward.
  • NET CHALLENGE is on the PC: practice battles and records.
  • GHOSTS is the third page of NET CHALLENGE (press R twice). START copies your team as a ghost code; SELECT pastes a friend's code; A fights their ghost, which plays the way they do. Swap codes and post times on the Ghost League page.
  • Items. Held items don't work in grid battles; NaviCust programs do their job. So an item that has a program becomes it as you get it (Leftovers, Charcoal, Protein and the other vitamins, Everstone, Soothe Bell and more), and a TM becomes 3 chips of its move. Berries and other battle-only items can be held only at the Battle Frontier, whose battles are Emerald's own.
  • EXP. SHARE is a key item: use it to turn it on or off. When it's on, the Pokémon that didn't fight get half a fighter's EXP.
  • CHIP TRADER is on the PC too. Put in 3 chips you don't need and get 1 random chip back. After the fifth badge, SELECT switches to the 10-chip trader, which gives rarer chips.

Saves and feedback

Save the way you do in Emerald. Everything is written to the game's folder:

  • pokeemerald.sav and pkbn.sav are your game. A backup of the second is kept as pkbn.sav.bak.
  • pkbn_log.txt is what happened this session.
  • pkbn_playlog.jsonl has one line per battle.
  • pkbn_replay.bin is your last battle. Watch it from NET CHALLENGE with SELECT.

In a browser the same files are kept by the browser, for that browser on that device only. Clearing the site's data, or a private window, loses them. Saves → Download a backup saves them all as one file, and Restore a backup brings them back, in any browser.

When something's off, send those files (in a browser, the backup file) along with what you were doing. The version is in the corner of the title screen.
